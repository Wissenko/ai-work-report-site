01 · READ THIS FIRST

05 · WHAT NOT TO HAND OVER

Before using AI, keep these boundaries clear.

Boundary

Priority decisions

AI can sort updates. Do not let it decide what matters most.

Why it matters here: Priorities depend on people, timelines, and business context.

Boundary

Escalation

AI can flag possible blockers. Do not let it decide when to escalate.

Why it matters here: Escalation affects relationships and expectations.

Boundary

Commitments

AI can draft updates. Do not let it promise dates or completion.

Why it matters here: Status updates can create expectations.

Boundary

Process authority

AI can clean up SOPs. Do not let it create process rules without approval.

Why it matters here: Process changes need ownership.

06 · YOUR FIRST TEST

Turn messy project updates into a risks, owners, and next-steps table.

The goal

Make updates easier to review before a status message or meeting.

Tools

Use a writing assistant to structure updates. Use your project tool, task system, or spreadsheet for the reviewed table.

If your company already provides an approved AI tool, start there. If not, use only safe, non-sensitive examples until you know the policy.

Steps

  1. Choose one internal project update.
  2. Paste safe notes from meetings, chats, or task comments.
  3. Ask AI to create a table of status, owner, risk, next step, and check first.
  4. Review owners, dates, and blockers.
  5. Use the reviewed table for your next status update.
Success looks like
  • Blockers are easier to see.
  • Owners and dates are clearer.
  • Unclear items are marked.
  • Status updates take less effort to prepare.
First-test rule

Keep the first test narrow, safe, and easy to review before using it on higher-risk work.

07 · WHAT THE TOOL NEEDS FROM YOU

AI will only be useful here if you give it the right raw material.

For the writing assistant, prepare
  • Project name or placeholder
  • Raw updates
  • Known owners
  • Known dates
  • Current status
  • Any known blockers
  • Output format
For the task or project system, prepare
  • Reviewed task name
  • Owner
  • Status
  • Due date
  • Risk level
  • Next step
Weak input

“Summarize these project updates.”

Better input

Turn these safe project updates into a status table. Do not mark anything complete unless I say it is complete. If an owner, date, or next step is unclear, mark it as ‘check first.’

Updates:
- [update]
- [blocker]
- [owner/date if known]
- [open question]

Leave out

  • Confidential project plans
  • Sensitive customer data
  • Financial details
  • Personnel issues
  • Anything not approved for AI tools

08 · WHAT YOU STILL OWN

The goal is not to hand your work to AI.

The goal is to stop doing the parts AI can prepare for you.

Responsibility

Priority

AI can organize updates. You still own what matters most.

Responsibility

Escalation

AI can flag risks. You still own whether something needs escalation.

Responsibility

Dependencies

AI can list dependencies. You still own whether they are accurate.

Responsibility

Commitments

AI can draft status language. You still own what dates or outcomes are promised.

Responsibility

Final status

AI can prepare a summary. You still own whether the status is true.

09 · YOUR PROMPTS AND TEMPLATES

Copy the prompt that matches the task you are starting with.

Replace anything in square brackets before you send.

Prompt 1 — Project updates to status table

Role/context: I coordinate operations or project work across tasks, owners, timelines, and updates. Task: Turn these raw updates into a status table I can review. Input: Project: [project or placeholder] Raw updates: [paste safe updates] Known owners: [owners] Known dates: [dates] Constraints: - Do not invent owners, dates, completion, or commitments. - Mark unclear items as “check first.” - Separate confirmed blockers from possible risks. - Keep the wording concise. Output format: Create a table with item, owner, status, risk or blocker, next step, due date, and what I should verify. Before you finish: List anything that needs clarification before I share this update.

Prompt 2 — Status update draft

Role/context: I prepare internal status updates. Task: Draft a short internal status update from the reviewed table. Input: Reviewed table: [paste table] Audience: [audience] Tone: [clear, factual, concise] Constraints: - Do not add new dates or commitments. - Separate done, blocked, and waiting. - Keep the message easy to scan. - Do not include sensitive information. Output format: Give me one status update and a check-before-sending list. Before you finish: Tell me what could be misread as a commitment.

10 · YOUR 7-DAY PLAN

Use one safe workflow for one week.

Day 1

Choose one internal project update.

Day 2

Run the status table prompt.

Day 3

Review owners, dates, blockers, and unclear items.

Day 4

Use the reviewed table in your normal project tool.

Day 5

Draft one internal status update.

Day 6

Compare with your usual update process.

Day 7

Decide whether to repeat this weekly.

If it works

Next, test meeting notes to action items and SOP cleanup.

If it does not work

The likely issue is mixed updates. Separate completed work, blockers, and questions before running the prompt.

Optional manager-safe explanation

“I’m testing AI to structure safe internal project updates. I’m not using it to set priorities, escalate issues, or make commitments without review.”
