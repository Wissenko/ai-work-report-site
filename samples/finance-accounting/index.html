05 · WHAT NOT TO HAND OVER

Before using AI, keep these boundaries clear.

Boundary

Numbers

AI can organize rows and questions. Do not let it decide whether numbers are correct.

Why it matters here: Finance work depends on accuracy.

Boundary

Approvals

AI can draft an approval request. Do not let it approve payment or coding.

Why it matters here: Approval authority matters.

Boundary

Sensitive financial data

AI can work from safe examples. Do not paste confidential financial details unless approved.

Why it matters here: Invoices, vendors, and payment data may be sensitive.

Boundary

Accounting treatment

AI can list questions. Do not use it to decide policy, treatment, or compliance.

Why it matters here: Accounting judgment needs approved guidance.

06 · YOUR FIRST TEST

Turn invoice and payment questions into an exception tracker.

The goal

Make open finance questions visible before they slow down approvals or month-end work.

Tools

Use a writing assistant to organize notes and draft questions. Use a spreadsheet for the final reviewed tracker.

If your company already provides an approved AI tool, start there. If not, use only safe, non-sensitive examples until you know the policy.

Steps

  1. Collect a safe set of invoice or payment questions.
  2. Remove confidential vendor, payment, or account details if needed.
  3. Ask AI to create an exception tracker.
  4. Review every amount, owner, and next step.
  5. Draft clarification emails only after review.
Success looks like
  • Each exception has a clear next step.
  • Missing information is marked.
  • Clarification emails are easier to send.
  • No numbers are used without your review.
First-test rule

Keep the first test narrow, safe, and easy to review before using it on higher-risk work.

07 · WHAT THE TOOL NEEDS FROM YOU

AI will only be useful here if you give it the right raw material.

For the writing assistant, prepare
  • Safe invoice issue notes
  • Amount placeholders if needed
  • Vendor or role labels
  • Known approval owner
  • Current status
  • What needs clarification
For the spreadsheet, prepare
  • Vendor or role
  • Invoice/date reference
  • Issue
  • Amount to verify
  • Owner
  • Status
  • Next step
Weak input

“Organize these invoices.”

Better input

Turn these safe invoice questions into an exception tracker. Do not invent amounts, approval status, coding, or payment timing. Mark unclear items as ‘check first.’

Notes:
- [Vendor or role] invoice has [issue]
- Amount to verify: [amount or placeholder]
- Approval owner: [person or role]
- Current status: [status]
- Question to ask: [question]

Leave out

  • Bank details
  • Full account numbers
  • Confidential vendor terms
  • Unapproved financial data
  • Payroll or employee-sensitive information

08 · WHAT YOU STILL OWN

The goal is not to hand your work to AI.

The goal is to stop doing the parts AI can prepare for you.

Responsibility

Numbers

AI can organize finance questions. You still own whether each number is correct.

Responsibility

Approval

AI can draft a request. You still own whether approval exists.

Responsibility

Accounting treatment

AI can help list questions. You still own the final treatment or escalation.

Responsibility

Vendor communication

AI can draft an email. You still own what is promised.

Responsibility

Sensitive data

AI can work from placeholders. You still own what is safe to paste.

09 · YOUR PROMPTS AND TEMPLATES

Copy the prompt that matches the task you are starting with.

Replace anything in square brackets before you send.

Prompt 1 — Invoice exception tracker

Role/context: I support finance and accounting work involving invoices, payment questions, spreadsheets, and approvals. Task: Turn my invoice notes into an exception tracker. Input: [Paste safe notes here.] Constraints: - Do not invent amounts, approvals, account coding, or payment timing. - Mark unclear items as “check first.” - Keep vendor questions clear and professional. - Do not include sensitive financial details. Output format: Create a table with vendor or role, issue, amount to verify, owner, status, next step, clarification question, and what I should check. Before you finish: List every number, approval, and commitment I should verify.

Prompt 2 — Clarification email draft

Role/context: I need to ask a clear finance clarification question. Task: Draft a short clarification email. Input: Recipient: [vendor or internal role] Issue: [issue] Known facts: [facts] Question: [question] Constraints: - Do not promise payment or approval. - Do not add numbers I did not provide. - Keep the tone professional and concise. - Mark missing information clearly. Output format: Give me one draft email and a checklist of what to verify. Before you finish: Tell me whether the draft creates any promise or assumption.

10 · YOUR 7-DAY PLAN

Test one useful AI workflow for one week.

Day 1

Choose one safe set of invoice questions.

Day 2

Run the exception tracker prompt.

Day 3

Review every number, owner, and status.

Day 4

Draft one clarification email.

Day 5

Add the reviewed tracker to your spreadsheet.

Day 6

Note where AI helped and where it guessed.

Day 7

Decide whether to use this for weekly exception review.

If it works

Next, test month-end checklist cleanup and variance explanation drafts after numbers are verified.

If it does not work

The likely issue is not enough structure in the notes. Add fields for vendor, issue, amount to verify, owner, and status.

Optional manager-safe explanation

“I’m testing AI to organize safe invoice questions and draft clarification emails. I’m not using it to validate numbers, approve payments, or decide accounting treatment.”
