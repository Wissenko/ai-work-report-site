YOUR AI WORK PLAN

Role: Customer Support Representative

Your best first AI test

Turn a messy customer ticket thread into a clear reply draft and escalation summary.

This is a strong first test because support work depends on clarity, empathy, policy accuracy, and clean handoffs.

01 · READ THIS FIRST

Your best first AI test is summarizing a messy ticket thread before drafting a customer reply.

Based on this sample interview, your best first AI test is using AI to summarize a messy ticket thread before drafting a customer reply.

This is the right place to start because:

  • Tickets often contain scattered details.
  • Customers need clear answers.
  • Escalations need concise context.
  • You can review the output before sending.
  • AI can prepare the reply without making policy decisions.
Use AI for

Summarizing the issue, drafting a reply, identifying missing information, and preparing an escalation note.

Keep yourself

Empathy, policy calls, refunds, account-specific decisions, customer promises, and final send.

This week, your goal is

Use AI on three non-sensitive tickets and compare the drafts against your normal replies.

05 · WHAT NOT TO HAND OVER

Before using AI, keep these boundaries clear.

Boundary

Policy decisions

AI can summarize the issue. Do not let it decide refunds, credits, exceptions, or eligibility.

Why it matters here: Support decisions can affect customers and company policy.

Boundary

Customer promises

AI can draft language. Do not let it promise timelines, fixes, or outcomes.

Why it matters here: Customers may rely on the wording.

Boundary

Sensitive customer data

AI can work from safe summaries. Do not paste private account data unless your company allows it.

Why it matters here: Ticket threads can contain personal or account-specific information.

Boundary

Final send

AI can prepare a reply. Do not send without checking tone, facts, and policy.

Why it matters here: The customer sees the final answer, not the draft.

06 · YOUR FIRST TEST

Turn a messy ticket thread into a clear reply draft and escalation summary.

The goal

Reduce time spent reconstructing the issue while improving clarity before sending.

Tools

Use your company-approved support AI or a writing assistant for safe summaries. Use your ticketing system for the final reply and internal note.

If your company already provides an approved AI tool, start there. If not, use only safe, non-sensitive examples until you know the policy.

Steps

  1. Choose a non-sensitive ticket.
  2. Remove private customer data if needed.
  3. Ask AI for an issue summary, missing facts, reply draft, and escalation note.
  4. Check facts, tone, policy, and promises.
  5. Send only after manual review.
Success looks like
  • You understand the thread faster.
  • The draft is easier to edit than starting from scratch.
  • Missing facts are visible.
  • Escalations are clearer.
First-test rule

Keep the first test narrow, safe, and easy to review before using it on higher-risk work.

07 · WHAT THE TOOL NEEDS FROM YOU

AI will only be useful here if you give it the right raw material.

For the writing assistant, prepare
  • Customer issue summary
  • Known facts
  • Relevant approved policy
  • What has already been tried
  • Desired tone
  • What not to promise
For the ticketing system, prepare
  • Final reviewed reply
  • Internal note
  • Escalation owner
  • Tags or status
  • Follow-up date
Weak input

“Reply to this customer.”

Better input

Summarize this safe ticket thread and draft a reply. Do not promise refunds, fixes, or timelines. Mark missing facts as questions to check.

Customer issue:
[summary]

Known facts:
[facts]

Approved policy:
[policy or link/summary]

Tone:
[empathetic, clear, concise]

Leave out

  • Full account numbers
  • Passwords
  • Payment details
  • Private customer information
  • Internal-only policy that cannot be shared
  • Anything company policy restricts

08 · WHAT YOU STILL OWN

The goal is not to hand your work to AI.

The goal is to stop doing the parts AI can prepare for you.

Responsibility

Empathy

AI can draft a reply. You still own whether the customer feels heard.

Responsibility

Policy

AI can reference policy text. You still own whether the policy applies.

Responsibility

Promises

AI can suggest wording. You still own timelines, refunds, fixes, and commitments.

Responsibility

Escalation

AI can summarize the issue. You still own whether escalation is needed.

Responsibility

Final send

AI can prepare the message. You still own the final customer-facing reply.

09 · YOUR PROMPTS AND TEMPLATES

Copy the prompt that matches the task you are starting with.

Replace anything in square brackets before you send.

Prompt 1 — Ticket thread to reply draft

Role/context: I work in customer support and need to answer tickets clearly and carefully. Task: Summarize this ticket and draft a customer reply I can review. Input: Customer issue: [paste safe summary] Known facts: [facts] Relevant policy: [policy] What has already been tried: [steps] Desired outcome: [outcome] Constraints: - Do not promise refunds, credits, fixes, or timelines. - If a fact is missing, mark it as “check first.” - Keep the tone empathetic, clear, and concise. - Do not include sensitive customer data. Output format: 1. Issue summary 2. Missing facts 3. Draft customer reply 4. Internal escalation note 5. What I should verify before sending Before you finish: List anything that could create a customer promise.

Prompt 2 — Escalation summary

Role/context: I need to escalate a customer issue to another team. Task: Create a concise internal escalation summary. Input: Issue: [issue] Customer impact: [impact] Steps tried: [steps] Evidence: [safe details] Question for internal team: [question] Constraints: - Do not add facts that are not included. - Separate confirmed facts from assumptions. - Keep it concise. - Remove customer-sensitive details unless needed. Output format: Create sections for summary, customer impact, steps tried, open question, and urgency. Before you finish: List what I should confirm before escalating.

10 · YOUR 7-DAY PLAN

Use one safe workflow for one week.

Day 1

Use AI to summarize one non-sensitive ticket thread.

Day 2

Add approved policy text and ask for a draft reply.

Day 3

Try the same process on a different ticket type.

Day 4

Use AI to prepare one internal escalation summary.

Day 5

Compare AI drafts against your normal replies.

Day 6

Note where AI created risk or saved effort.

Day 7

Decide which ticket types are safe enough for this process.

If it works

Next, test macro cleanup and escalation templates.

If it does not work

The likely issue is missing policy context. Add approved policy and stricter “do not promise” instructions.

Optional manager-safe explanation

“I’m testing AI on safe ticket summaries and draft replies. I’m not using it to decide refunds, policy exceptions, or customer promises, and I review everything before sending.”
