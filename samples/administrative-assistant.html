Where AI fits first

01 · READ THIS FIRST

Where AI fits first: turning rough daily notes into a clean follow-up list.

Based on this sample interview, the best first place for AI is turning rough daily notes into a clean follow-up list.

This is the right place to start because:

  • Follow-ups are part of your daily work.
  • The output is easy to review.
  • Missing one request creates visible friction.
  • The task can be tested with safe internal notes.
  • AI can organize and draft without deciding for you.
Use AI for

Organizing rough notes, drafting short internal reminders, and turning scattered requests into a clear task table.

Keep yourself

Priority, tone, real deadlines, sensitive details, external messages, and any promise made on behalf of the company.

This week, your goal is

Use AI on one internal follow-up list, review every item, and decide whether this saves enough manual organizing to become part of your routine.

AI is not yet attached to a repeated part of the work. That does not mean your judgment is behind. It means the organizing, drafting, and follow-up tracking around your judgment are still mostly manual.

The gap shows up in follow-ups. This is where other people may start using AI to move faster: turning rough notes into tasks, drafting reminders, and cleaning up internal messages.

05 · WHAT NOT TO HAND OVER

Before using AI, keep these boundaries clear.

Boundary

Sensitive details

AI can help organize safe notes. Do not use it for private employee information, customer data, passwords, financial details, or anything your company policy does not allow.

Why it matters here: Your work touches many people and requests.

Boundary

External messages

AI can draft a message. Do not let it decide what gets sent outside the team.

Why it matters here: A draft can accidentally create a promise or use the wrong tone.

Boundary

Deadlines and commitments

AI can list possible deadlines from your notes. Do not let it invent due dates or commitments.

Why it matters here: Your follow-ups depend on accuracy.

Boundary

Scheduling decisions

AI can draft a scheduling message. Do not let it decide timing, conflicts, or priority.

Why it matters here: Calendar changes affect real people, rooms, meetings, and expectations.

06 · YOUR FIRST TEST

Use AI to turn rough daily notes into a clean follow-up list you still review.

The goal

Reduce manual tracking and make small requests easier to review before they get missed.

Tools

Use a writing assistant for organizing notes and drafting reminders. Use your normal task system for the final reviewed list.

If your company already provides an approved AI tool, start there. If not, use only safe, non-sensitive examples until you know the policy.

Steps

  1. Collect rough notes from meetings, email, chat, and quick conversations.
  2. Remove private or sensitive details.
  3. Ask the writing assistant to turn the notes into a follow-up table.
  4. Review every deadline, owner, message, and “check first” item.
  5. Move the reviewed items into Planner, To Do, a spreadsheet, or your normal task system.
Success looks like
  • You catch more small follow-ups.
  • The list is easier to review than your rough notes.
  • The tool marks unclear items instead of guessing.
  • You spend less time rebuilding the day from memory.
First-test rule

Keep the first test narrow, safe, and easy to review before using it on higher-risk work.

07 · WHAT THE TOOL NEEDS FROM YOU

AI will only be useful here if you give it the right raw material.

For the writing assistant, prepare
  • Rough daily notes
  • Known deadlines
  • Person, team, or role labels
  • Whether the message is internal or external
  • Your preferred tone
  • The table format you want
For your task system, prepare
  • Final reviewed task names
  • Owners or roles
  • Real due dates
  • Priority
  • Notes
  • Items that need confirmation
Weak input

“Make this organized: badges, lunch, deck, laptop, parking.”

Better input

Turn these safe internal notes into a follow-up table. Do not invent deadlines. If something is unclear, mark it as ‘check first.’

Notes:
- [Person or role] asked about [request], maybe by [date]
- Send [count or item] to [vendor or team]
- [Person or role] needs [document] after [condition]
- Ask [team] whether [item] shipped
- Remind [team] about [logistics detail]

Leave out

  • Passwords
  • Private employee issues
  • Customer data
  • Confidential financial information
  • Legal or policy-sensitive details

08 · WHAT YOU STILL OWN

The goal is not to hand your work to AI.

The goal is to stop doing every preparatory step by hand when AI can help draft, organize, summarize, or check.

Responsibility

Priority

AI can list follow-ups. You still own what is urgent, because you know the people, timing, and business context.

Responsibility

Tone

AI can draft reminders. You still own the tone, because a reminder can sound too cold, too casual, or too pushy.

Responsibility

Deadlines

AI can pull deadlines from your notes. You still own whether the deadline is real, guessed, or missing.

Responsibility

Sensitive details

AI can organize safe notes. You still own what should stay private.

Responsibility

Final send

AI can prepare a message. You still own what gets sent.

09 · YOUR PROMPTS AND TEMPLATES

Copy the prompt that matches the task you are starting with.

Replace anything in square brackets before you send.

Prompt 1 — Rough notes to follow-up list

Role/context: I handle administrative follow-ups across email, meetings, chat, scheduling, and office requests. Task: Turn my rough notes into a follow-up list I can review. Input: [Paste safe rough notes here.] Constraints: - Do not invent deadlines, names, owners, or commitments. - If something is unclear, mark it as “check first.” - Keep messages professional, clear, and friendly. - Do not include sensitive details. - Do not make decisions for me. Output format: Create a table with these columns: person or role, request, deadline, next step, draft message, what I should verify. Before you finish: List any deadlines, commitments, or details I should check before using this.

Prompt 2 — Meeting notes to task list

Role/context: I take notes from meetings and need to turn them into clear follow-up tasks. Task: Turn these meeting notes into a task list. Input: Meeting type: [meeting type] Participant roles: [roles] Notes: [paste safe notes] Task system: [Planner, To Do, spreadsheet, other] Constraints: - Do not invent owners or due dates. - Mark unclear tasks as “needs clarification.” - Keep task names short. - Separate decisions from follow-ups. - Do not include sensitive details. Output format: Create a table with task, owner or role, due date, priority, notes, and what to verify. Before you finish: List anything that needs clarification before I add it to my task system.

10 · YOUR 7-DAY PLAN

Test one useful AI workflow for one week.

Day 1

Use AI on one internal follow-up list.

Day 2

Try the same prompt with a different set of notes.

Day 3

Add deadlines, owners, and “check first” items.

Day 4

Move the reviewed list into your normal task system.

Day 5

Try one routine internal email draft.

Day 6

Compare what AI got right, what it missed, and what you had to fix.

Day 7

Decide whether this belongs in your daily routine.

If it works

Your next best tests are meeting notes to task lists, routine internal email drafts, and office announcement cleanup.

If it does not work

The likely issue is the input. Try cleaner notes, clearer labels, and a stricter rule not to invent deadlines.

Optional manager-safe explanation

“I’m testing AI on internal follow-up organization to help with drafting and organizing. I’m not using it for sensitive details or external messages, and I’m reviewing deadlines, tone, commitments, and final wording before anything is used.”
