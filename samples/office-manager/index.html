01 · READ THIS FIRST

Your best first AI test is turning scattered office requests into a weekly issue tracker.

Based on this sample interview, your best first AI test is turning scattered office requests into a weekly issue tracker.

This is the right place to start because:

  • Requests come from email, chat, hallway conversations, and vendor messages.
  • Many items are small, but they become visible when missed.
  • The output is easy to review.
  • AI can organize requests without deciding urgency or spend.
  • You can test it with safe internal notes.
Use AI for

Grouping office requests, drafting vendor follow-ups, identifying missing details, and creating a simple status table.

Keep yourself

Urgency, spending decisions, vendor commitments, employee-sensitive details, facilities decisions, and final messages.

This week, your goal is

Use AI to organize one week of office requests into a reviewed issue tracker.

The gap shows up when memory becomes the office request tracker.

Your current positionSporadic.

For this role, the risk is not just time. It is visibility. People notice when the office supply, room, repair, badge, lunch, or vendor issue was forgotten.

Based on this sample interview: Your best opportunity is to stop using memory as the main office request tracker.

05 · WHAT NOT TO HAND OVER

Before using AI, keep these boundaries clear.

Boundary

Spending decisions

AI can organize vendor notes. Do not let it approve costs, purchases, or commitments.

Why it matters here: Office managers may touch budgets, invoices, and vendor quotes.

Boundary

Employee-sensitive details

AI can help rewrite safe internal messages. Do not paste private employee concerns or HR-related details.

Why it matters here: Office requests sometimes include personal or sensitive context.

Boundary

Vendor commitments

AI can draft follow-ups. Do not let it promise delivery dates, payment, or approval.

Why it matters here: A vendor email can create expectations.

Boundary

Facilities access

AI can summarize a repair issue. Do not include access codes, badge details, or security-sensitive information.

Why it matters here: Office operations can involve physical access and security.

06 · YOUR FIRST TEST

Turn scattered office requests into a weekly issue tracker.

The goal

Stop relying on memory for small requests and make open office items easier to review.

Tools

Use a writing assistant to organize notes. Use a spreadsheet, task system, or ticket board for the final reviewed tracker.

If your company already provides an approved AI tool, start there. If not, use only safe, non-sensitive examples until you know the policy.

Steps

  1. Collect office requests from one week.
  2. Remove employee-sensitive, access-sensitive, or financial details.
  3. Ask AI to group them into a tracker.
  4. Review urgency, owner, status, and missing details.
  5. Move the reviewed tracker into your normal system.
Success looks like
  • Open office issues are visible.
  • Requests have next steps.
  • Missing details are clearly marked.
  • You spend less time searching through messages.
First-test rule

Keep the first test narrow, safe, and easy to review before using it on higher-risk work.

07 · WHAT THE TOOL NEEDS FROM YOU

AI will only be useful here if you give it the right raw material.

For the writing assistant, prepare
  • Rough office request notes
  • Vendor names or role labels
  • Known dates
  • Current status
  • Whether the request is internal or vendor-facing
  • Any approval needed
For your tracker, prepare
  • Request title
  • Owner
  • Status
  • Due date
  • Next step
  • Missing detail
Weak input

“Organize office stuff from this week.”

Better input

Turn these safe office request notes into a weekly issue tracker. Do not mark anything as approved unless I wrote approved. If a date, owner, or status is unclear, mark it as ‘check first.’

Notes:
- [Team] asked about [office request]
- Vendor said [issue/update]
- Need to check [supply/facility/event item]
- [Person or role] asked whether [logistics question]

Leave out

  • Access codes
  • Badge details
  • Private employee concerns
  • Financial details not approved for AI
  • Vendor contract terms
  • Anything company policy restricts

08 · WHAT YOU STILL OWN

The goal is not to hand your work to AI.

The goal is to stop doing the parts AI can prepare for you.

Responsibility

Urgency

AI can sort requests. You still own what is urgent, because you know what affects the office day.

Responsibility

Spending

AI can organize vendor information. You still own purchase or budget decisions.

Responsibility

Vendor promises

AI can draft follow-ups. You still own what the company promises to vendors.

Responsibility

Employee privacy

AI can rewrite safe messages. You still own what should stay private.

Responsibility

Final status

AI can prepare a tracker. You still own whether each status is accurate.

09 · YOUR PROMPTS AND TEMPLATES

Copy the prompt that matches the task you are starting with.

Replace anything in square brackets before you send.

Prompt 1 — Office request tracker

Role/context: I manage office requests, vendors, supplies, facilities, and internal logistics. Task: Turn my rough notes into a weekly office issue tracker. Input: [Paste safe notes here.] Constraints: - Do not mark anything as approved unless I say it is approved. - Do not invent deadlines, owners, costs, or vendor commitments. - Mark unclear items as “check first.” - Do not include sensitive employee or access information. Output format: Create a table with request, source, owner, status, next step, due date, and what I should verify. Before you finish: List any approvals, costs, vendor promises, or sensitive details I should check.

Prompt 2 — Vendor follow-up draft

Role/context: I coordinate office vendors and need clear follow-up messages. Task: Draft a vendor follow-up from my notes. Input: Vendor: [vendor] Issue: [issue] Known facts: [facts] Desired outcome: [outcome] Constraints: - Do not promise payment, approval, or timing. - Keep the tone professional and clear. - Ask for confirmation where needed. - Do not include private internal details. Output format: Give me one draft email and a “check before sending” list. Before you finish: Tell me what I should verify before sending.

10 · YOUR 7-DAY PLAN

Test one useful AI workflow for one week.

Day 1

Collect one week of office request notes.

Day 2

Remove sensitive details and run the tracker prompt.

Day 3

Review owners, urgency, missing details, and status.

Day 4

Move reviewed items into your normal tracker.

Day 5

Try one vendor follow-up draft.

Day 6

Compare the AI tracker with your usual notes.

Day 7

Decide whether this should become your weekly office request review.

If it works

Next, test vendor follow-up drafts and event logistics checklists.

If it does not work

The likely issue is unclear inputs. Add status labels like new, waiting, approved, blocked, or check first.

Optional manager-safe explanation

“I’m testing AI to organize safe office request notes into a tracker. I’m not using it for employee-sensitive information, access details, vendor commitments, or spending decisions.”
