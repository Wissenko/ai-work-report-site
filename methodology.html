Methodology

How your AI Work Plan is made.

The report is designed to be practical, grounded in your interview, and specific to your work. It is built to show where AI fits your actual job — not to give you a generic AI advice sheet.

1. The interview starts with your real work

The interview asks about your role, recurring tasks, current tools, examples, review steps, sensitive information, and the work you still do mostly by hand.

The goal is to capture enough concrete detail to show where AI can help, where it may create risk, and which first workflow is worth testing.

2. The transcript is analyzed for work patterns

The report looks for repeated tasks, structured inputs, frequent outputs, quality standards, risk points, and work that could be easier to draft, organize, summarize, check, or lightly automate.

  • Repeated work gets priority over one-off tasks.
  • Tasks with a clear input and output are easier to test.
  • Tasks with serious consequences need a stronger review step.

3. Tool recommendations start with category fit

The report recommends categories first: general AI assistant, meeting notes tool, research assistant, workflow tool, or role-specific tool.

Specific tools are included only when they fit the person's workflow, existing tools, and privacy constraints.

4. Benchmarks are used only when they change the advice

Benchmarks should explain the person's position, not decorate the report. A statistic appears only when it helps answer what to do next.

If a benchmark does not clarify the diagnosis or first test, it should be left out.

5. The first workflow is deliberately practical

The report chooses one first workflow to test. The best first workflow is frequent, structured, low enough risk with review, and connected to a real frustration.

The purpose is to help you start this week, then decide from evidence whether that AI use belongs in your routine.

6. The report separates preparation from decisions

AI can draft, summarize, organize, compare, check, and turn notes into a starting point. The report also names what you should still decide, verify, approve, or review.

This matters when work involves customers, money, deadlines, reputation, private company information, or messages sent externally.

7. What the report does not claim

  • It does not guarantee time savings.
  • It does not replace company policy or legal guidance.
  • It does not say every task should be automated.
  • It does not predict whether your job will be replaced.
  • It does not recommend tools that could not be connected to the transcript.
